Custos

Privacy policy

Last updated 11 October 2026

Custos is a private productivity application with a single user, who also built and runs it. It is not offered to the public and has no sign-up. This policy describes how Custos accesses, uses, stores, shares and deletes data from that user’s Google account. It is based on how the software currently works.

Google data Custos accesses

Custos requests read-only access. It cannot send, delete, label or otherwise change email, and it cannot create, edit or delete calendar events.

  • Gmail (gmail.readonly). Custos reads a small number of recent email threads, at most five at a time. It normally reads only threads the owner has marked with a Gmail label for review, and the owner can also run a manual search. For each thread it reads the messages’ headers (such as subject, sender, recipient and date) and their text. It also checks the account profile to confirm the connection works.
  • Google Calendar (calendar.readonly, calendar.events.readonly). Custos reads events on the owner’s primary calendar for today and tomorrow, when a screen needs them. From each event it keeps only the title, start and end time, all-day and busy/free status, and location. Descriptions, attendees, organisers and meeting links are discarded as soon as they arrive. Custos also reads the calendar’s name and time zone.

How Custos uses it

  • To suggest tasks from email. Each suggestion shows a title, a short description and a time estimate. The owner can approve, edit or discard it.
  • To show the original email next to a suggestion. That text is fetched from Gmail when it is opened and is not stored.
  • To notice when the owner has replied to an email linked to a task, so Custos can suggest marking the task done.
  • To show the day’s schedule, to write a short evening review of the day, and to compare tomorrow’s free time with the tasks planned for it.

Custos uses Google data only for these features. Custos does not use it for advertising, does not sell it, and does not itself use it to train AI models; what MiniMax may do with the data it receives is described below. Custos contains no analytics or tracking.

What Custos stores, and where

Custos runs on a computer the owner controls. Its database and files are kept there. The app can be reached only from the owner’s private network, and only after the owner signs in. Custos adds no encryption of its own to stored data; it relies on the access controls and security of that computer.

  • From Gmail: thread identifiers and links; a short excerpt of up to 512 characters made from the subject and message text, with email addresses, phone numbers and long numbers removed (names and other text are not removed); AI-written task suggestions and their edit history; and, for replies the owner has sent, the redacted subject, the recipient’s email domain and a short redacted excerpt. Full email bodies are not stored.
  • From Google Calendar: titles and times of events, saved as part of the evening review and planning records, along with event identifiers and the generated review text. Event locations are not stored.
  • Google sign-in tokens: stored in a file on the server with restricted file permissions, so Custos can keep its access without asking again.

Backups of the database and the token file are taken every six hours. On the server they are kept for up to eight weeks. Encrypted copies are kept on a second computer the owner controls for up to twelve weeks. Backups taken by hand or before maintenance can be kept longer, until they are deleted.

Until October 2026 Custos ran on hosted services (Fly.io and Neon). They are no longer in use, but they still hold a copy of data from that period, including Google sign-in tokens, as a temporary fallback.

Custos’s logs record identifiers, counts and errors rather than email or calendar content, and they stay on the server. The Custos iPhone app does not keep its own copy of email or calendar content.

Sharing with service providers

Custos shares Google-derived data with the following services, only to provide the features above.

  • MiniMax (an AI model provider). When Custos’s AI features are switched on, it sends MiniMax:
    • the redacted subject and up to about 4,000 characters of redacted text from each email thread it reviews, with the sender’s name, the date and the thread’s identifiers, to find possible tasks;
    • when a task is suggested, up to 12,000 characters from the last five messages in that thread, with each message’s subject, sender and date. This text is not redacted. It is used to write the description and estimate the time needed;
    • a suggestion’s title, description and estimate, when the owner asks the AI to revise it;
    • titles and times of today’s and tomorrow’s calendar events, and task titles (some of which come from email), for the evening review. Event titles that look sensitive, for example about health or family, are removed first.
    Custos does not set any data-retention or training preferences with MiniMax, and MiniMax does not document a way to do so. MiniMax’s API terms (effective 30 March 2026) allow it to use the content it receives to provide, maintain, develop and improve its services, and to keep it for as long as it considers necessary or the law allows. Custos therefore cannot guarantee that this data is not used to improve MiniMax’s AI models.
  • MeisterTask (task manager). After the owner approves a suggestion, Custos creates a task with a title of up to 120 characters and notes of up to 800 characters. These are written by the AI from the email and may describe its content. Custos does not copy the email text, the sender’s address or a link to the email into the task.
  • Apple Push Notification service. Notifications contain only fixed wording and counts, never email or calendar content.

Apart from the services named in this policy, no Google data is sent to any AI provider or other third party. Data is not sold, and no person other than the owner can use Custos or read its stored data.

Retention and deletion

  • Custos keeps stored data until the owner deletes it; there is no automatic expiry. Backups expire on the schedule above.
  • Disconnecting Gmail or Google Calendar in Custos deletes its stored sign-in tokens. It does not delete data already stored and does not cancel access on Google’s side.
  • To cancel Custos’s access at any time, remove it on your Google Account’s third-party connections page. Custos can then no longer read Gmail or Calendar, and it deletes its stored token the next time Google rejects it.
  • Custos has no in-app control for deleting stored email or calendar data. Because the owner is the only user, the owner deletes it directly from the database and lets the backups expire. Tasks in MeisterTask are deleted in MeisterTask.

Changes and contact

This policy will be updated when Custos changes how it handles Google data, and the date above will change. Questions can be sent to the support email shown on Custos’s Google sign-in screen.
